Why the Kumbh comes to Nashik

The Kumbh Mela rotates between four holy cities: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. According to the Puranas, when the devas and asuras churned the ocean (Samudra Manthan), drops of amrit, the nectar of immortality, fell at these four places.

The Nashik mela is called Simhastha because it is held when Jupiter (Guru) is in Leo (Simha rashi). That happens about once every 12 years. The last Simhastha here was in 2015–16, and the next will be around 2039.

The Kumbh Mela is on UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2017).

What makes Nashik's Kumbh unique

  • Two bathing centres. Vaishnava akhadas bathe at Ramkund in Nashik city. Shaiva akhadas bathe at Kushavarta Kund in Trimbakeshwar, about 30 km away.
  • The Godavari, "Dakshin Ganga". The river rises at Brahmagiri hill near Trimbakeshwar and flows through Nashik.
  • Ramayana land. Panchavati is where Lord Rama, Sita and Lakshmana are believed to have lived during their exile.
  • A Jyotirlinga. Trimbakeshwar is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva.

Rituals explained

A quick guide to what you will see and hear during the mela.

Amrit Snan

The holy dip on fixed auspicious days. The akhadas bathe first, in a grand procession; devotees bathe after. It is believed to wash away sins.

Akhadas

Orders of sadhus and sanyasis. Vaishnava akhadas worship Lord Vishnu, and Shaiva akhadas worship Lord Shiva. Each has its own camp, flag and procession.

Naga sadhus

Ascetics of the Shaiva akhadas who renounce worldly life. Their procession on snan days is among the most watched sights of the Kumbh. Photograph them only with their permission.

Dhwajarohan

The hoisting of the dharma flag that formally opens the Simhastha. It took place on 31 October 2026 and the flag stays up until July 2028.

Goda Aarti

The evening aarti to the Godavari at Ramkund, with lamps, bells and chants. You can attend it any day of the year, not only during the mela.

Parva snan

Other auspicious bathing days between the Amrit Snans, such as Rishi Panchami and Purnima. They are less crowded and good for families.

Plan your trip

The Kumbh falls in the monsoon (July–September). Book early for snan dates; come on regular days if you are with children or elders.

How to reach

  • By train: Nashik Road railway station, on the Mumbai–Delhi and Mumbai–Kolkata lines.
  • By air: Nashik (Ozar) airport, about 20 km from the city, or Mumbai and Pune airports with onward road travel.
  • By road: About 165–170 km from Mumbai and about 210 km from Pune.
  • To Trimbakeshwar: About 30 km from Nashik by road. Expect diversions and shuttle buses on snan days.

Where to stay

  • Hotels and homestays fill months ahead for the Amrit Snan dates.
  • Staying a little outside the centre (Nashik Road, Gangapur Road, Trimbak Road) can be cheaper, but check how you will get to the ghats when roads close.
  • Dharamshalas and ashrams near Panchavati and Trimbakeshwar take pilgrims; book directly and confirm by phone.
  • Official tent cities, if announced, will be listed by the Kumbh administration.

Best days to go

  • See the processions: Amrit Snan days. Expect huge crowds and long walks.
  • Families and elders: parva snan days or regular days.
  • Before or after the peak: visit the temples between November 2026 and June 2027 for calm darshan.

    In the crowd

    • Agree on a meeting point (a temple gate or a numbered pole) before entering the ghat area.
    • Put a note with your name, phone number and hotel in every child's and elder's pocket.
    • Follow police barricades and one-way routes. Never push or stop on steps.
    • Bathe only in marked, guarded areas. The river can run fast in the monsoon.

    Health and comfort

    • Drink sealed or boiled water, and carry ORS.
    • Keep medicines and prescriptions in a waterproof pouch.
    • Wear non-slip footwear; ghat steps get slippery.
    • Keep your phone charged and carry a power bank. Networks get slow on snan days, so save offline maps.

    Questions people ask

    When is the Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027?

    The Simhastha began on 31 October 2026 and ends on 24 July 2028. The main Amrit Snan days are 2 August, 31 August and 11–12 September 2027.

    Where does the bathing take place?

    At Ramkund in Panchavati, Nashik, and at Kushavarta Kund in Trimbakeshwar. Vaishnava akhadas bathe at Ramkund and Shaiva akhadas at Kushavarta.

    Can families with small children attend?

    Yes, but avoid the three Amrit Snan days if you can. Parva snan days and regular days are calmer and still spiritually significant.

    Is there an entry fee for the Kumbh?

    There is no ticket for the mela or the holy dip. Be careful of anyone selling "special passes"; check official sources first.

    How far is Trimbakeshwar from Nashik?

    About 30 km by road. Allow extra time on snan days because of diversions.

    What is the difference between Shahi Snan and Amrit Snan?

    They are the same ritual. The official name is now Amrit Snan.
