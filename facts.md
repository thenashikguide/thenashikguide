## Why & history
- Simhastha = held when Jupiter (Guru) is in Leo (Simha rashi). One of the 4 Kumbh sites:
  Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik.
- Legend: Samudra Manthan; drops of amrit fell at the 4 sites.
- Kumbh Mela is on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list (2017).
- Unique to Nashik: two bathing centres – Ramkund (Nashik) for Vaishnava akhadas and
  Kushavarta Kund (Trimbakeshwar) for Shaiva akhadas.
- Godavari is called "Dakshin Ganga". It rises at Brahmagiri near Trimbakeshwar.

## Places to cover
- Trimbakeshwar Jyotirlinga – one of the 12 Jyotirlingas; ~30 km from Nashik. Kushavarta Kund nearby.
- Brahmagiri hill – source of Godavari; trek/steps.
- Ramkund, Panchavati – where Lord Rama is believed to have bathed; evening Godavari (Goda) Aarti.
- Panchavati – linked to Ramayana exile: Sita Gufa, Tapovan, five banyan trees.
- Tapovan – associated with Lakshmana and Shurpanakha (name "Nashik" linked to "nasika", nose).
- Kalaram Mandir – black stone Rama idol, built late 18th century; site of Dr. Ambedkar's 1930 temple-entry satyagraha.
- Kapaleshwar Mahadev – near Ramkund.
- Sundarnarayan Temple, Naroshankar Temple (famous bell), Muktidham (white marble, Gita verses on walls).
- Saptashrungi Devi, Vani – one of the Shakti Peethas of Maharashtra (~60 km).
- Anjaneri – believed birthplace of Lord Hanuman (near Trimbakeshwar).
- Pandavleni Caves – ancient Buddhist rock-cut caves (approx. 2nd century BCE onward).
- Someshwar temple & waterfall.

## Travel basics
- Nashik is ~165–170 km from Mumbai and ~210 km from Pune.
- Nashik Road railway station; Nashik (Ozar) airport.
- Kumbh is in monsoon (Jul–Sep): carry rain gear, waterproof footwear, light cotton clothes.
- Hotels fill months ahead for snan dates. Regular (non-snan) days are calmer – best for families & elders.
